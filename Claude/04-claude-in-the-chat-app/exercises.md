# 04 Claude in the Chat App: Exercises

**How to do these:**

- Use the chat app at [claude.ai](https://claude.ai), or the desktop app. A free account is enough for every exercise here.
- Write your answers in `journal/ch04.md`, inside your `claude-practice` folder.
- The app's menus change often. If a button isn't where an exercise says, look around for the same idea, or ask Claude: "Where do I find projects in this app?"
- Don't upload anything private. Use your own course notes or made-up data.
- Try on your own first. Only open a hint if you've been stuck for a while.
- When you're done, ask Claude to check your journal for the chapter.

---

## Exercise 1 (Easy): Hand over the real thing

1. Pick one `notes.md` file from a course you've already finished, such as a JavaScript or Python chapter.
2. Upload it to a new chat.
3. Ask three questions that can only be answered from the file. For example: "Which common mistakes does this chapter list?"
4. Open the file yourself and check one answer word by word.

Then take a screenshot of any error message or any piece of code on your screen. Upload it and ask Claude what it shows.

In your journal:

- Was the answer you checked exactly right?
- What did Claude get from the screenshot that you'd have had to type out otherwise?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look for the **+** button or a paperclip near the message box. You can also drag a file onto the chat window, or paste an image straight from your clipboard.

</details>

## Exercise 2 (Easy): Search, then check the source

1. Pick a tool you use, such as React, Django, or PostgreSQL.
2. In a new chat, ask: "What is the latest stable version of this, and what is one notable change in it? Search the web."
3. Click at least one citation and find the sentence that supports Claude's answer.

In your journal:

- Which website did the answer come from? Is it the official site?
- Did the page really say what Claude said it did?
- Compare with your result from chapter 02, exercise 2, where Claude answered from memory. What changed?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

If Claude answers without searching, look for a web search option under the **+** button, or simply ask it to search. An official source is usually the tool's own website or its page on GitHub. A random blog is not.

</details>

---

## Exercise 3 (Medium): Build a study project

Make a project that acts as your tutor for one course in this folder.

1. Create a new project. Name it after the course, for example "JavaScript tutor".
2. Write **project instructions**. Include who you are, how you want things explained, and one clear rule about exercises, such as "give hints, never full solutions".
3. Add two or three of your course's `notes.md` files as **project knowledge**.
4. Start a chat in the project and ask something the notes cover.
5. Start a **second** chat in the same project. Paste in an exercise and ask for the answer outright.

In your journal:

- Did the second chat follow your "hints only" rule, even though you never repeated it?
- Did Claude use your notes? How could you tell?
- Change one instruction and test again. How quickly did the behaviour change?

<details>
<summary>Hint 1</summary>

Good instructions are specific and short. "Be helpful" changes nothing. "When I share an exercise, give me one hint at a time and wait for me to try" changes a lot.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Hint 2</summary>

To check whether Claude used your notes, ask about something worded in an unusual way in them, such as an everyday comparison the notes use. If Claude repeats that comparison, it read the file.

</details>

## Exercise 4 (Medium): Make something you can keep

Ask Claude to build an artifact that helps you revise a topic you're learning. Pick one:

- a one-page cheat sheet,
- a set of ten flashcards you can click through,
- a short quiz that tells you your score.

Then improve it **three times** with follow-up messages. Each message should ask for one specific change.

In your journal:

- Paste your first prompt and your three follow-ups.
- Which follow-up made the biggest improvement?
- Look at the sharing options without sharing anything. Who can see your artifact right now?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

If Claude answers in the chat instead of opening a separate panel, ask for it directly: "Make this as an artifact." If that doesn't work, check your settings for a capability about creating files and make sure it's switched on.

</details>

## Exercise 5 (Challenge): Audit your own settings

Most people never open the settings. You're going to read them all.

1. Find the **memory** settings. Is memory on? Open the list of what Claude remembers about you. Is anything there wrong, or something you'd rather it didn't keep?
2. Find the **privacy** setting about improving Anthropic's models. Read what it says. Decide what you want it to be, and set it.
3. Find where to write **instructions for Claude** that apply to all chats. Write three lines about how you like to work.
4. Start an **incognito chat**. Tell it a made-up fact. Then open a normal new chat and ask about that fact.
5. Open the list of **connectors**. Don't connect anything. Just read what's available.

In your journal:

- Write down each decision you made in steps 1 to 3, with one sentence saying why.
- What happened in step 4, and why?
- Pick one connector you might find useful one day. What could it read? What could it *do*? What's the worst thing that could happen if it did the wrong thing?
